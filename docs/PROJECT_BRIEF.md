# Aurora Confinement Website Project Brief

## Purpose

Create a credible, fast, accessible website for Aurora Confinement ahead of the Nordic Fusion Forum in Copenhagen on 19 October 2026.

The immediate purpose is to:

- Explain Aurora Confinement clearly to fusion researchers, industry leaders, potential customers, strategic partners, advisers, and early-stage investors.
- Turn in-person conversations into qualified follow-up conversations.
- Validate demand for exhibition models and research collaboration.
- Present the team professionally without overstating its legal status, maturity, funding, partnerships, or institutional support.

This is a public, non-confidential website. It must not disclose patent-sensitive engineering details.

## Organisation status

Aurora Confinement is currently:

- A student-led research initiative bringing together talent from DTU and KU.
- Based in the Copenhagen/Lyngby area of Denmark.
- Pre-incorporation and does not yet have a CVR number.
- Pre-funding.
- Preparing for possible incubation support and advice concerning intellectual property.

Do not describe Aurora Confinement as an incorporated company, university spinout, university-affiliated company, DTU Skylab-incubated company, funded startup, or formal partner of any institution unless the team later supplies written confirmation.

An appropriate temporary description is:

> Aurora Confinement is a student-led research initiative bringing together talent from DTU (Technical University of Denmark) and KU (University of Copenhagen).

An appropriate temporary footer status is:

> Aurora Confinement is a student-led project in formation, based in Kongens Lyngby, Denmark.

## Mission and positioning

Aurora Confinement aims to make stellarator science more tangible and accessible. The team is developing physical tools that can support fusion education, public engagement, university teaching, and, over time, experimental research.

The long-term ambition is to broaden participation in stellarator research and contribute to the development of commercially useful fusion technology.

The website must use the correct spelling: **stellarator**.

Possible primary positioning:

> Making stellarator research tangible and accessible.

Possible supporting statement:

> Aurora Confinement is a multidisciplinary student-led team bringing together talent from DTU and KU, developing physical stellarator models for education and exhibition while exploring research-scale platforms for universities and fusion R&D.

This wording is provisional and should remain easy to edit.

## Product directions

### Tabletop exhibition models

Physical stellarator models intended for uses such as:

- University teaching and outreach.
- Fusion conferences and exhibitions.
- Science centres and museums.
- Public engagement and technical communication.

The website may invite visitors to request indicative pricing or register institutional interest. It must state clearly when a model is still in development and must not imply immediate availability unless confirmed by the team.

Suggested call to action: **Request indicative pricing**.

### Research-scale platforms

Early-stage experimental stellarator platforms intended to expand practical access to stellarator research for universities, startups, and other R&D organisations.

Use cautious terminology such as "research-scale platform," "experimental stellarator system," or "research system" until the team confirms that "research reactor" is technically and legally appropriate.

Suggested call to action: **Discuss a research partnership**.

Do not publish detailed geometries, manufacturing methods, simulations, performance specifications, enabling technical details, or other potentially novel information without an explicit IP review.

## Audiences

### Researchers

Researchers need to see:

- A scientifically grounded rationale.
- The intended research or educational use cases.
- Honest technical limitations and technology readiness.
- The team's relevant disciplines and capabilities.
- Concrete opportunities for research collaboration.

### Industry and potential customers

Industry visitors need to understand:

- Which problem is being addressed.
- The two distinct product directions.
- The intended users and applications.
- Why accessible stellarator hardware may be valuable.
- How to request pricing, discuss a pilot, or explore a partnership.

### Early-stage investors

Potential investors need to see:

- A credible initial product and customer group.
- A larger long-term opportunity.
- A capable and committed multidisciplinary team.
- Honest development status and milestones.
- Evidence of customer discovery and institutional interest.
- A responsible approach to IP, safety, and claims.

The site may welcome investment conversations but must not solicit payment or claim that an investment round is formally open unless the team later confirms this.

## Website structure

### Home

The homepage should answer immediately:

1. What is Aurora Confinement?
2. What is the team building?
3. Who is it for?
4. What should the visitor do next?

Recommended sections:

- Hero with primary positioning and two clear calls to action.
- Two product-direction cards.
- Why accessible stellarator research matters.
- Honest current-stage statement.
- Short team introduction.
- Latest news.
- Collaboration call to action.

Primary calls to action:

- Discuss a collaboration.
- Register interest.
- Request indicative pricing.
- Discuss a research partnership.

Do not use **Buy now** unless a genuine product can be ordered and fulfilled under appropriate commercial terms.

### Technology

- Explain stellarators in accessible language.
- Present the tabletop and research-platform tracks separately.
- Describe intended use cases and development stage.
- Provide enough scientific substance to establish credibility without publishing confidential or patent-sensitive details.
- Invite researchers and industry specialists to discuss requirements.

### Vision

- Explain why access to fusion education and experimental hardware is limited.
- Describe how broader access may support education, workforce development, experimentation, and collaboration.
- Connect the near-term product direction with the long-term fusion mission.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives or guaranteed fusion timelines.

### About and team

- Tell the project's origin story.
- Introduce the multidisciplinary student-led team.
- Include team names, roles, biographies, photographs, and external profile links only after each person approves publication.
- State the relationship to DTU and KU accurately without implying endorsement.
- Explain values such as scientific integrity, safety, responsible engineering, and openness to collaboration.

### News

Use local Markdown content rather than a CMS for the initial release.

Suitable topics include:

- Introducing Aurora Confinement.
- Prototype or model progress.
- Event participation.
- Demonstrations and presentations.
- Confirmed partnerships, awards, programmes, or publications.

Do not launch with invented or placeholder achievements.

### Join

- Explain desired disciplines and roles.
- State expected time commitment and location expectations.
- State clearly whether positions are unpaid, student-project, thesis-related, or funded.
- Avoid collecting more applicant information than necessary.
- Add an applicant privacy notice before accepting CVs or applications.

### Contact

Offer a professional email address and a minimal contact or interest form.

Recommended fields:

- Name.
- Professional email.
- Organisation, optional for general contact.
- Organisation type, where relevant.
- Area of interest.
- Intended application or short message.
- Expected timeframe, optional.
- Indicative budget range, optional.

The form must state that an expression of interest is non-binding and is not an order. No payment is taken.

### Nordic Fusion Forum landing page

Create a focused page at `/nff` for visitors met at Nordic Fusion Forum 2026.

Suggested heading: **Continue the conversation**.

Interest categories:

- Exhibition or educational models.
- Research collaboration.
- Early customer or pilot opportunity.
- Technical advising.
- Investment conversation.
- Other partnership.

Internally associate submissions with the source `nff2026`. A short `/nff` URL should be suitable for a QR code.

### Legal and trust pages

- Privacy notice.
- Accessibility statement.
- Clear project/legal status in the footer.
- Contact details.

Do not generate legal guarantees or claim certified compliance. Mark text requiring organisational or legal confirmation.

## Demand validation

Measure the interest funnel honestly:

1. Aggregate `/nff` or relevant page views.
2. Call-to-action activations.
3. Completed expressions of interest.
4. Relevant organisations represented.
5. Follow-up meetings.
6. Pricing or pilot requests.
7. Non-binding letters of intent.

Do not describe clicks as customers, orders, or purchases. Do not disclose the identity of an interested organisation to investors or display its logo without written permission.

Prefer privacy-preserving, first-party aggregate counts with no cross-site tracking, advertising identifiers, visitor profiles, or persistent analytics cookies. Do not retain full IP addresses or detailed user-agent strings for analytics.

## Visual direction

The desired style is:

- Dark and cinematic.
- Scientific and technically serious.
- Large, confident typography.
- Generous spacing and uncluttered layouts.
- Restrained use of the aurora-inspired cyan, violet, blue, and magenta palette.
- Subtle depth and motion inspired by magnetic confinement and auroral light.
- Professional enough for researchers, industrial leaders, and investors.

Avoid generic sci-fi dashboards, stock photographs of unrelated tokamaks, excessive glow, constant animation, or visual effects that harm legibility.

All motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Initial technical direction

- Next.js (App Router, static export).
- TypeScript in strict mode.
- React only as required by Next.js; client components kept to the minimum interactivity needed.
- Static generation for all possible pages.
- Local, build-time CSS; no Tailwind CDN.
- Locally hosted fonts, icons, scripts, and images.
- Markdown-based news articles.
- Minimal client-side JavaScript.
- No database for the initial public site.
- No accounts, login, payments, advertising, or behavioural tracking.

The contact form may use a minimal server-side endpoint that validates the submission and forwards it to email without retaining the message in an application database.

## Privacy, security, and accessibility baseline

- Collect only data required to respond to an enquiry.
- Show a privacy notice beside or immediately linked from forms.
- Do not use a consent checkbox merely to acknowledge the privacy notice.
- Use separate, optional, unticked consent only for unrelated marketing.
- Validate all form data on the server.
- Apply rate limiting and a honeypot or similarly proportionate anti-spam control.
- Never expose secret keys to the browser or commit them to Git.
- Use HTTPS in production.
- Define a restrictive Content Security Policy and other appropriate security headers.
- Minimise and redact logs; establish a retention period.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA and semantic HTML.
- Ensure full keyboard operation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, useful alternative text, labelled forms, and helpful validation messages.
- Test responsive behaviour and accessibility before launch.

The website and generated text must not claim GDPR, legal, security, accessibility, or regulatory certification.
